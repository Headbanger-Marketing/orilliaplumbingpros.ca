Plan the request

Comparing how things work now with how they used to

Some changes are easy to notice and hard to describe: hot water may run out sooner, a drain may empty more slowly, or a toilet may refill differently. For an Orillia home, write down how the item behaved before, using old notes, photos or household routines, then how it behaves now. List anything that changed in between, such as more people, a new appliance or earlier work, so a provider can compare the two.

  • Earlier behaviour

    Write what you remember about how the item worked before, and note whether that memory comes from a record or from habit.

  • Present behaviour

    Describe what happens now in plain terms, such as how long a fixture takes to empty, refill or recover, without guessing at the cause.

  • Changes in between

    List household changes since then, such as more occupants, a new appliance, a past repair or a different routine.

Before you send

Photos That Help Before the First Call

Photos of the right parts save a lot of back-and-forth. Take these before you send the request and have them ready for the follow-up.

  • Water heater: the label with the model and capacity, the vent connection at the top of the tank, and any rental sticker or agreement.
  • Blocked drain: which fixtures back up, whether it only happens on the lowest floor, and the floor drain or cleanout cap if you can find it.
  • Leak: the stain or damp patch, the water meter dial with every tap closed, and any recent renovation nearby.
  • Sump pump: the pit with the lid off, where the discharge pipe leaves the house, and any battery or water-powered backup.
  • Older supply pipe: the line beside the water meter. Copper is orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey with threaded joints, and lead is dull grey and shows bright silver when scratched lightly with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Orillia Request

Should I describe how my Orillia plumbing worked before, even if I only remember it?

Yes, and say it is a memory. Mark which details come from old notes, photos or a bill, and which come from habit, because a respondent can weigh a record differently from a recollection. A rough comparison is still useful, as long as it does not claim to know the cause.

What household changes should I mention when something in my Orillia home behaves differently?

Mention anything that altered demand or routine since the earlier behaviour: more occupants, a new dishwasher or washer, a changed schedule, a renovation, earlier plumbing work or a long absence. These are context, not conclusions, and the provider decides what they mean.

Does a note comparing my Orillia plumbing then and now mean a provider has agreed to the job?

No. A sent request confirms nothing about coverage, availability, price or timing. Our enquiry team reads it first, and any provider must later confirm the address, the work, their qualifications, the written scope, pricing and scheduling. Nothing is booked until those points are agreed.

Can one request cover a slow drain and a weaker shower in my house?

Yes, if you describe each change on its own line: what it did before, what it does now and when you noticed. Separate lines let the respondent see whether the two changes share a cause or are unrelated, without you having to guess. Unconnected items can also be sent as separate requests.

Can a before-and-after comparison tell a provider what is wrong in my Orillia home?

Not by itself. A comparison describes what changed; it does not show why. It gives the provider a baseline, a present description and the household changes in between, and the provider may then propose checks that can separate the possible causes. Treat it as a starting point, not a diagnosis.

What should I do if a fixture in my house starts backing up while I wait for a reply?

Stop using a fixture that backs up, and do not force a valve that will not turn. If you smell gas near a heater, leave and contact the gas supplier or emergency services from outside. Note what you saw and when, and add it to your request for the enquiry team.
